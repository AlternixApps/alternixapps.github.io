Privacy Policy

This policy explains how QR Dot Lab handles information when you create, customize, save, or share QR codes.

Effective date and last updated: 29 July 2026

1. Who we are and scope

QR Dot Lab (package ID com.alternix.dotlab) is an Android application published by Alternix (“Alternix”, “we”, “us”, or “our”). This Privacy Policy applies to the QR Dot Lab application and the QR Dot Lab pages on this website.

QR Dot Lab creates static QR codes. The current version works locally on your device and does not require registration, an account, or a connection to an Alternix server.

2. Privacy summary

  • QR content and designs are processed on your device.
  • The app does not send QR content, location, Wi-Fi details, or history to Alternix.
  • The app has no account system, cloud synchronization, or server-side component.
  • The current version contains no Firebase, Analytics, Crashlytics, AdMob, or other advertising or tracking SDK.
  • The application does not declare the Android INTERNET permission and does not make network requests.

3. Information processed on your device

You may choose to enter information to encode into a QR code. Depending on the QR type, this can include:

  • plain text, website addresses, telephone numbers, and email addresses;
  • social-network profile or content links;
  • Wi-Fi network name (SSID), password, security type, and hidden-network setting;
  • geographic latitude and longitude;
  • cryptocurrency asset, network, recipient address, optional amount, and QR format;
  • vCard details such as name, telephone number, email address, organization, job title, website, and postal address;
  • visual settings, including colors, gradients, module and finder styles, frame text, export size, and error-correction level;
  • a logo image selected by you or a built-in icon.

This information is used only to create and display the QR code and to provide the local history and export features described below. Alternix does not receive it.

Important: a QR code contains the information you put into it. Anyone who can scan a QR code you display, print, save, or share may be able to read that information.

4. Local history and settings

QR history

When you create an eligible QR code, QR Dot Lab stores a local history entry in an SQLite database within the app’s private storage. An entry can contain the QR category, encoded payload, creation or update time, favorite status, full visual design, export size, and any logo embedded in that saved design.

The app keeps up to 100 non-favorite entries. Favorite entries are retained until you remove them from favorites, delete them, clear the app’s data, or uninstall the app. Creating the same category and payload again updates its timestamp and design instead of creating a duplicate. You can delete individual entries or clear all non-favorite entries from the History screen.

Wi-Fi QR codes are excluded from history. Their SSID and password are not written to the history database.

Preferences

The selected light or dark theme and application language are stored locally in the app’s private preferences. No preference is uploaded to Alternix.

Backups

Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled for QR Dot Lab’s private app data in the current version.

5. Android permissions

QR Dot Lab declares the following permissions:

  • ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION and ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION — requested only after you tap the location auto-fill action, and also used where Android requires location permission to read the connected Wi-Fi network name.
  • NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES — requested on supported Android versions when you ask the app to auto-fill details of the connected Wi-Fi network.
  • ACCESS_WIFI_STATE — used to check Wi-Fi state and read locally available information about the current connection.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — used to inspect the current Wi-Fi connection through Android system APIs. It does not give the app Internet access.

The app does not request background location. It does not request camera, contacts, microphone, advertising ID, broad photo access, or broad file-storage permissions.

6. Location and connected Wi-Fi auto-fill

Location

Location is accessed only after you explicitly choose to auto-fill coordinates and grant Android’s runtime permission. The app may use a recent on-device location (up to five minutes old) or request a current location from Android. The coordinates populate the form locally. If you then create a geolocation QR code, the coordinates become part of that QR content and may be stored in local history. They are never sent to Alternix.

Wi-Fi

Connected Wi-Fi information is accessed only after you explicitly choose auto-fill and grant any required Android permission. The app attempts to read the SSID and security type. Android does not provide the saved Wi-Fi password to third-party applications, so the password must be entered manually. Wi-Fi data remains in the active form and generated QR code and is not saved to QR history.

If you deny a permission or disable the related system service, you can enter coordinates and Wi-Fi information manually.

7. Logos, saving, and sharing

Selected logo images

When you choose a logo, Android’s system picker gives QR Dot Lab access only to the selected file. The app reads and processes the image locally, limits input files to 25 MB, and scales the working copy to a maximum of 512 × 512 pixels. If the design is saved to local history, an encoded copy of the prepared logo is stored with that design.

Saving files

QR Dot Lab uses Android’s system document interface for PNG, SVG, JPEG, PDF, EPS, and TIFF exports. You select the destination and the system or destination provider writes the file there. The app does not require broad storage permission. Exported files remain at the location you selected until you delete them.

Sharing

When you choose Share, the app creates a temporary PNG in its private cache and exposes that single file through Android’s secure FileProvider with temporary read access. Android’s share sheet lets you choose the recipient application. Once you share a file, the chosen application or service handles it under its own privacy terms. Alternix does not receive the file.

8. Third-party services and network activity

The current QR Dot Lab build uses Android and AndroidX components and the ZXing Core library for on-device QR encoding. It contains no Firebase, Google Analytics, Crashlytics, AdMob, or other analytics, advertising, attribution, or tracking SDK, and it makes no network requests.

Google Play and the Android operating system may independently process app-store, installation, security, diagnostic, or device information under Google’s own terms. Applications or storage providers you choose through Android’s save and share interfaces may also process the files you send them. These services are not operated by Alternix.

Future advertising: AdMob is not included in the current version. Before any version containing an advertising SDK is released, this policy and the Google Play Data safety declaration must be updated to describe the SDK and its data practices.

9. This website

The Alternix GitHub Pages site is static. Its source code does not use cookies, analytics, advertising scripts, external fonts, third-party JavaScript libraries, tracking pixels, or forms that collect personal information. The light/dark theme switch works only in the current page session and is not stored in your browser.

GitHub hosts this site and may process standard request information, such as an IP address and browser details, for delivery, security, and operational purposes under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

10. Support emails

If you email alternix.apps@gmail.com, we receive the email address, message, and attachments you choose to provide. We use them to respond to your request, investigate an issue, and maintain necessary support records. Email is handled through the email providers used by you and Alternix and may be processed in countries other than your own.

You may ask us to delete a support conversation when it is no longer needed, subject to any legal obligation to retain it.

11. Retention, deletion, and security

Local history and settings remain on the device according to the rules in Section 4. You can delete history entries in the app. Clearing QR Dot Lab’s app data in Android settings or uninstalling the app removes its private local database, preferences, and cache. Files you previously exported or shared are outside the app’s private storage and must be deleted from their destination separately.

QR Dot Lab reduces exposure by processing information locally and disabling backup of private app data. Local data is nevertheless protected by your device, Android version, lock-screen settings, backups outside the app, and the applications with which you choose to share files. Avoid putting confidential information in a QR code unless you understand how it will be displayed and shared.

Application error logging records only an operation name and exception type in the Android system log; user-entered QR content is not intentionally included. There is no remote crash-reporting service in the current version.

12. Your choices and privacy rights

You control whether to grant optional runtime permissions, use auto-fill, save or share a QR code, add a logo, keep a history entry, or contact support. Permission choices can be changed in Android settings.

Because Alternix does not receive app-created QR content or local history, we cannot view, correct, export, or delete that device-only information for you. For personal information you send directly by email, applicable law may give you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or object to processing. Contact us to make a request.

13. Children’s privacy

QR Dot Lab is a general-purpose QR creation tool and is not specifically directed to children. The app does not knowingly collect or transmit children’s personal information to Alternix. Parents and guardians should help children avoid encoding or sharing sensitive personal information in QR codes.

We may update this policy when the app’s features, permissions, SDKs, data practices, or legal obligations change. The updated version will be posted at this URL with a new “last updated” date. Material changes will be reflected in the app and Google Play disclosures where required.
